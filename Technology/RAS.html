Built Around Advanced Engineering

Mechanical Filtration
Precision drum filters down to 40 microns continuously extract suspended solids, uneaten feed, and large particulate matter before they can break down and degrade water quality.
Biofiltration
Advanced Moving Bed Biofilm Reactors (MBBR) house highly specific nitrifying bacteria that convert toxic ammonia into harmless nitrate, ensuring a safe biological environment.
Degassing
Aeration stripping towers forcibly remove excess carbon dioxide and nitrogen gases produced by high-density fish respiration, preventing pH crashes.
Oxygenation
High-pressure oxygen dissolution cones precisely inject pure oxygen back into the water column, supporting massive biomass densities safely.
Disinfection
Commercial-grade Ultraviolet (UV) and Ozone treatment loops destroy free-floating bacteria, viruses, and pathogens, maintaining absolute biosecurity.
Recirculation
Polished, perfectly balanced water is pumped back into the culture tanks—recovering up to 99% of total system volume for a continuous closed-loop cycle.

Executive Briefing & Technical Q&A

How does RAS differ from traditional flow-through aquaculture?

Direct Answer

Flow-through systems pull water from a source, pass it through tanks once, and discharge it as effluent. RAS captures the water, mechanically and biologically purifies it, and returns it to the tanks—reusing up to 99% of system volume.

What limits production density in RAS engineering?

Direct Answer

Density is bound entirely by engineering capabilities—specifically oxygen transfer efficiency and biofiltration capacity to process ammonia. Aero Intelli systems are engineered to lift these ceilings significantly.

How is biosecurity maintained in closed-loop aquaculture?

Direct Answer

By decoupling the farm from external water bodies and deploying commercial UV/Ozone loops, the vectors for pathogen entry are virtually eliminated, drastically reducing the need for antibiotics.

Can the system operate autonomously without continuous manual intervention?

Direct Answer

While human oversight is required for biological observation, our automation layer (SCADA) handles real-time parameter corrections, oxygen dosing, and water balancing autonomously.

The future of aquaculture depends on systems that produce more with fewer resources while maintaining absolute environmental responsibility.
